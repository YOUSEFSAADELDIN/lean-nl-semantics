Does it follow?

Checking natural-language entailment with a proof assistant, not a language model.

Below, a small system decides whether one English sentence follows from one or more others, and proves it. Each result is checked by Lean 4, the same kind of interactive theorem prover used to verify mathematics, applying a short, fixed list of logical principles. Nothing is guessed by a language model; every step corresponds to an already-proved statement the Lean kernel accepts.

2. Five principles

Every example on this page is closed by one of five principles, or by two of them applied in sequence. None was written to fit a particular sentence; each is a general statement, proved once, about how the relevant English determiners (every, some, at least n, at most n) behave.

4. Coverage

The system above is deliberately narrow. Its scope is the monotonicity and cardinality behavior of four quantifiers over two predicates: not anaphora, tense, comparatives, or the many other phenomena a broader benchmark tests.
